The second-to-last row carries the comparison. At Red Sky she met thirty higher-ranked players and came out level — fourteen beaten, fifteen ahead of her, one tied. At Ron Moore she met twenty and beat every one she could. The two groups are not comparable opposition: Red Sky’s thirty included Jasmine Koo 12th, Vanessa Borovilos 13th, Bailey Shoemaker 14th, Eunseo Choi 20th and Kylie Chong 24th; at Ron Moore the best rank in the field was Foong Zi Yu at 123rd. Going 19–0 against one group and 14–15 against the other is not a contradiction. It is the same player measured against two different standards.

Which was better depends on what is being measured, and the honest answer is that the two measure different things. Against the field in front of her, Ron Moore by a factor of two. For ranking points, Ron Moore again, because WAGR pays for winning. As evidence about her ceiling — whether she can play at the level that gets Denver to a regional — Red Sky is the better week, and that is why it leads this page. Red Sky proved she belongs in that company. Ron Moore proved she wins when she is the best player there.

One number cuts against the win. Her raw score was two better at Ron Moore, but the field scored 5.3 strokes worse over the same 54 holes. On a neutral setup she probably played nearer her Red Sky level than the 209 suggests.

The complementary fact is just as stark. Every top-ten finish of her career — now T1, T3, T5, T5, T6, T8 — has come in a field rated 293 or below, the Ron Moore win included. Until Red Sky she had never combined a strong field with a strong finish; the two lived in separate halves of this table. That is the whole argument of this page, and it is now a property of the data rather than a claim about it.

Two smaller readings. The FAU Paradise Invitational in February 2026 drew ten major-conference programs — the second-highest count here — and still rated only 318 for power, a reminder that WAGR counts the players who show up, not the badges on the bags; a Power Four program travelling with its second five is not a strong field. The .500 record is a season-long goal, not a footnote. Every tournament is a head-to-head result as well as a finish, and the line is drawn at the end of conference play. Denver is 28–16–1 (.636) so far this fall — 9–3 at Redbird, 10–9 at Red Sky, 9–4–1 at Ron Moore — against 53–77–2 (.409) for all of last season. That is the single clearest measure of how different this team already looks, and it is worth protecting: the Wallace Classic alone puts fifteen higher-ranked teams in a twenty-team field, and two weeks of that pull the percentage back toward the line quickly. Win the conference and none of it matters; finish second and it is the gate that has to be open before the ranking is even consulted.

One number argues the at-large is not absurd. At Red Sky, Denver shot 860 and beat Colorado head-to-head, 860 to 867 — and Colorado was the last team into the 2026 NCAA field, at No. 51. One event is not a season, but it is the right kind of evidence. The likelier route remains the conference title — and the gap there is 9.3 strokes a round on paper, 6.3 with Farbu counting, and smaller still on any week Denver's worst round is not eighteen behind. This group has the pieces to put Denver back inside the top 50: five counting scorers between 72.50 and 74.33, a sixth in Hamilton who was tied second on the team at Ron Moore last October, and four Elite or Strong fields still on the schedule to be rated in.

The risk, and the four months

The risk is depth. Five play and the best four count, so one bad round a day is free — Denver used that at Red Sky, dropping an 80 on the Tuesday. What the format does not cover is two at once, or a lineup that cannot be filled. That question is live every week. February is only where a wrong answer costs the most: the Wallace Classic is first back after the winter, and it was the deepest field on last season’s schedule.

Those four months are also the season’s quietest advantage. October to February is rest after three events in three weeks, a full winter of building with no scorecard due on Friday, and open amateur events for anyone who wants to keep competing — which is also where world-ranking points are available while the college schedule is dark. A team that treats the gap as preparation rather than a pause arrives in February sharper than it left in October.

One thing worth confirming with the conference. An NCAA automatic qualifier requires six sponsoring members that have competed together for two years, with no waivers — the Patriot League lost its women's golf AQ in July 2026 for being one program short. The WCC will have exactly six, and three of them — Denver, Seattle U and Augusta — are recent additions. Nothing published addresses whether that affects the WCC's bid. If the AQ did not hold, every team in the conference would be an at-large case.

What the fall has shown

She is the team’s most reliable scorer and, since late September, its most dangerous one. Across nine tournament rounds she has not gone lower than 67 or higher than 78, a seven-shot band, while at Red Sky and Ron Moore alone teammates ranged from 68 to 83 — but the floor moved: nothing above 74 since Redbird.

The fall divides in two. Through Redbird she was steady and over par, third on the team, without a round at par or better. Then eleven days in late September and early October produced a T23 in the second-strongest field of her career, a T1 in her own event, a career-low round, a career-low total and her first college title. Red Sky proved she belongs in that company; Ron Moore proved she wins when she is the best player in the field. The two are different claims and she now has both.

For Denver the fall reads the same way one layer out. 291.4 a round against 299.3 last season, a first sub-280 in 29 months, two finishes better than the ranking they carried in, and a lineup that is still changing shape — Farbu counting for the first time at Ron Moore and finishing T4, with Hamilton and Capizzi beating counting scores from outside it. The program was 39th in the country two years ago and 113th last season. Nothing here yet says which of those numbers the team is closer to. It says the question is open again, which it was not in April.

What would settle it is not another good week in a weak field. It is the Jim West Challenge on October 18–19, the Wallace Classic in February, and the West Coast Conference Championship in April — the three places where a top-50 season and a conference title are actually decided.
