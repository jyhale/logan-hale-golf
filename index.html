Two years ago Denver was 39th in the country and finished sixth of twelve at the East Lansing Regional, one place from the NCAA Championship. Last season was the floor of a two-year slide, not the level of the program. Through two events this fall they are averaging 292.0 a round against 299.3 last season — already back at the 2024–25 mark, with the lineup not yet settled.

The pieces for a return are already on the roster, not in a projection. Five scorers sit inside 74.33 this fall — Louw 72.50, Hale 72.67, Farbu 73.83, Richani 74.17, Capizzi 74.33 — and Hamilton, who was tied second on the team at Ron Moore last October, has not played an event yet. The 113 was set without Louw or Richani in the lineup and without Farbu counting. With four Elite or Strong fields still to be rated in, this group has the pieces to put Denver back inside the top 50 — which would be the program's second-best season in four years: past last year's floor and past the 68, short of the 39.

The conference, April 23–25

Six teams, one week. Pepperdine beat Denver by 28 at Red Sky — but 18 of those strokes came in one round, the other two were inside seven, and putting Farbu in the counting five cuts the margin to 19. The title also goes for less than that suggests: Pepperdine won the 2026 championship at 865, one over par, and Denver shot 860 three weeks ago. Projected third — but second through fourth is a scramble, and the number that wins this conference is one Denver has already shot. The title is a real target, not a long shot.

Winning the conference is the clean route: the champion takes an automatic bid to an NCAA regional and is exempt from everything else. And the bar is lower than “win the league” sounds — Pepperdine took the 2026 title at 865, one over par, and Denver shot 860 at Red Sky three weeks ago against a field with nine top-50 teams in it, without Farbu counting and with Hamilton at home. Pepperdine is the team to beat, not a team out of reach.

There is also a floor to protect. Since 2024–25, any team taken at large must finish the regular season .500 or better head-to-head against Division I opponents — only the conference champion is exempt. Denver is 19–12 (.613) through two events: 9–3 at Redbird, 10–9 at Red Sky. Every tournament is a head-to-head record as well as a finish, and staying above that line is what keeps the second route open at all.

That second route is the national ranking, near 51st in 2026 against Denver's 113th — and the roster case for closing that gap is set out above. What this schedule adds is the chance to: four of the nine events are Elite or Strong, which is where ranking points are actually earned. The October ranking is the first read on how much of that 113 still applies.
